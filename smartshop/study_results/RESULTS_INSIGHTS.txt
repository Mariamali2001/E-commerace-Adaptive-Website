7. P-VALUES — WHAT TO USE, WHAT TO AVOID
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Do report a small planned set. Do not p-hack every persona×mood cell
(many cells have n=1–4).

Planned:
  (1) SUS vs 68 (one-sample t + Wilcoxon) — primary usability claim.
  (2) SUS mobile vs desktop on joined rows — exploratory, not randomised.
  (3) Camera Yes vs 50% binomial — user confirmation, not lab accuracy.
  (4) AWEQ-5 Yes vs No — wait until camera n is larger (~75).

Current tests:
  - SUS vs 68 (one-sample t): n=111  p=0.0  Cohen d=0.82  (H0: mean SUS = 68. Significant p means scores differ from the common benchmark.)
  - SUS vs 68 (Wilcoxon signed-rank): n=111  p=0.0    (Non-parametric check of the same H0 (SUS is often skewed).)
  - SUS mobile vs desktop (Mann-Whitney U): n=76  p=0.0109  rank-biserial=-0.36; mobile n=26 mean=88.8; desktop n=50 mean=78.7  (Exploratory. Joined rows only. Not a randomised device assignment.)
  - Camera confirm Yes vs 50% (binomial): n=35  p=0.0019  Yes=27/35=77.1%; 95% CI [59.9%, 89.6%]  (User confirmation of bridged guideline mood, not pixel-level FER accuracy vs a lab gold set.)
  - AWEQ-5 camera Yes vs No: n=19  p=  Yes n=15; No n=4 — too small for a stable test  (Wait for Tuesday camera N (~75) before treating this as inferential.)

α = 0.05, two-sided. No multiple-comparison correction (only these tests).
If p < 0.05 write “statistically significant in this sample,” not “proved.”
